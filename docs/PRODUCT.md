# Scouta — Product Overview & Policy Positioning

This document is the source of truth for what Scouta is, how its virtual
economy works, and how it should be positioned to platforms (Apple App
Store / Google Play) and to users. Any feature work should stay consistent
with the design principles in section 18 unless a deliberate product/legal
decision changes them.

## 1. Product overview

Scouta is a football scouting and talent-discovery game.

Players build a virtual portfolio of professional footballers using a
fictional in-game currency called SC. The objective is to identify players
whose future performance may justify a higher valuation, manage a
portfolio intelligently, and compete with other users based on scouting
performance.

Scouta uses investment-style mechanics as its gameplay system, but it is
not intended to provide financial services, real-money investing, sports
betting, or gambling.

The core fantasy is:

> Can you spot football talent before everyone else?

## 2. The in-game currency

Scouta uses SC, a fictional virtual currency.

- Every new user receives exactly 1,000 SC.
- SC has no real-world monetary value.
- SC cannot be withdrawn for cash.
- SC cannot be exchanged for real money.
- SC cannot be transferred to another user.
- Users do not purchase SC with real money.
- Users do not receive cash or real-world prizes based on their SC balance.
- SC exists solely as a gameplay resource.
- Users cannot lose or win real money through Scouta.

## 3. Player ownership

Users can allocate SC to football players at their current Scouta
valuation.

The system represents this as fractional virtual holdings.

For example: a user may allocate 100 SC towards a player currently valued
at 100 SC per share.

The user is not purchasing an actual footballer's economic rights, shares,
securities, or ownership. The player remains entirely fictional within
Scouta's game economy.

## 4. How player prices work

Player prices are determined by Scouta's football valuation engine.

Prices are intended to respond to football evidence including:

- Real match performance
- Minutes played
- Goals and assists
- Underlying performance metrics such as xG/xA
- Changes in starting/bench status
- Confirmed transfers
- Confirmed injuries
- Promotion and relegation
- Genuine tournament performance
- Other relevant football-performance information

Prices are updated on a scheduled basis.

User trading activity does not determine player prices. A large number of
users buying a player does not mechanically increase that player's price.
Likewise, users selling a player cannot mechanically crash their price.

The valuation engine is therefore independent of user demand.

## 5. No betting or wagering

Scouta does not allow users to place bets on football matches or other
uncertain events.

There are:

- No betting odds
- No match wagers
- No accumulator bets
- No sportsbook
- No casino games
- No roulette/dice/card mechanics
- No jackpot
- No randomised cash-equivalent prizes
- No betting against other users

The user is making a scouting decision about a footballer's virtual
valuation, rather than wagering on the outcome of a sporting event.

## 6. No real-money prizes

Users cannot convert successful Scouta performance into cash.

There is no:

- Cash-out mechanism
- Withdrawal mechanism
- Real-money prize pool
- Cash competition
- Cryptocurrency reward
- Gift-card reward
- Financial return
- Real-world ownership of football assets

SC and Scouta portfolio value exist solely within the game.

## 7. Selling

Users can sell some or all of their virtual holdings at the player's
current Scouta price.

The resulting SC remains inside the game. Selling therefore does not
represent a cash payout, a redemption of money, a withdrawal, or a
financial investment return. It is a gameplay mechanic used to manage the
user's virtual portfolio.

A 2% in-game transaction fee applies to purchases and/or sales.

## 8. How users succeed

Scouta rewards users for making successful scouting decisions. Examples
include:

- Identifying an undervalued player
- Recognising a player whose role is improving
- Identifying strong underlying performance before headline statistics catch up
- Holding a player through a genuine breakthrough
- Managing portfolio concentration
- Taking profits intelligently
- Avoiding overvalued or declining players

Success is therefore intended to represent football knowledge and
scouting skill, rather than luck-based wagering.

## 9. Scout Potential

Scouta includes a Scout Potential rating. This is a scouting/opportunity
signal rather than a gambling multiplier.

Potential does not directly increase a player's price. A player with high
Potential can remain inexpensive if real football evidence does not
validate that potential.

The intended gameplay loop is:

> Spot potential → make a scouting decision → wait for real football
> evidence → see whether your judgement was correct.

## 10. Competition

Scouta may include leaderboards comparing users based on portfolio
performance.

Competition is based on virtual gameplay performance. Users do not stake
money against one another. There is no real-money prize pool attached to
portfolio performance.

Casual and Professional modes use the same underlying economy and rules.

## 11. Randomness

Scouta's core player valuation system is not based on random outcomes.
Player prices are intended to respond to football data and confirmed
football events. A player does not rise or fall because a random number
generator determines that the user won or lost a wager.

## 12. Anti-manipulation architecture

Player prices are deliberately disconnected from user trading volume.
This means: 10,000 users buying a player does not automatically increase
their price.

The purpose is to prevent users from manipulating the virtual market
through coordinated buying or selling. The game instead relies on a
centrally controlled football valuation engine.

## 13. What Scouta is NOT

Scouta is not intended to be:

- A sportsbook
- A betting application
- A casino
- A financial investment platform
- A securities trading platform
- A cryptocurrency application
- A real-money gaming platform
- A fantasy betting product
- A gambling product
- A platform for winning real-world money

## 14. Appropriate product positioning

The preferred description of Scouta is:

> Scouta is a football scouting and talent-discovery game where you build
> a virtual portfolio of players and compete based on your ability to
> identify football talent before its value changes.

The phrase "football stock market" may be useful as an informal
explanation when describing the concept to people, but the official
product positioning should emphasise: **football + scouting + talent
discovery + strategy + portfolio management** rather than gambling
terminology.

## 15. Key policy questions for Apple

Before launch, Scouta should seek clarification on whether its virtual
player-portfolio mechanic is considered simulated gambling under Apple's
applicable rules.

The specific question to ask App Review / Developer Support:

> Scouta is a football scouting game using non-purchasable virtual
> currency. Users receive 1,000 SC for free, use SC to acquire fractional
> virtual positions in football players, and player values change
> according to real-world football performance and statistics. SC has no
> monetary value and cannot be purchased, withdrawn, transferred, or
> exchanged for anything of value. There are no cash prizes, betting
> odds, match wagers, or randomised gambling mechanics. Users compete
> based on portfolio performance and football scouting decisions.
>
> Would this mechanic be considered simulated gambling under Apple's App
> Store Review Guidelines or age-rating system, and are there any
> additional requirements we would need to meet for distribution on the
> App Store?

Apple's documentation confirms that age-rating information is required
and that its questionnaire includes content descriptors and capabilities
that determine the resulting rating. Apple's documentation also
specifically refers to licensing requirements for real-money gaming and
gambling, which is distinct from simply having a virtual in-game economy.

## 16. Key policy questions for Google Play

The equivalent question should be asked of Google:

> Scouta is a football scouting game using non-purchasable virtual
> currency with no real-world value. Users receive 1,000 SC for free and
> use it to manage virtual holdings in football players. Player values
> change according to football performance and statistics. SC cannot be
> purchased, withdrawn, transferred, or exchanged for real-world value.
> There are no cash prizes, sports bets, betting odds, or casino
> mechanics.
>
> Would this gameplay be considered gambling or simulated gambling under
> Google Play's current policies, and are there any additional
> requirements or restrictions that would apply?

Google currently maintains separate policies covering real-money
gambling, games and contests, alongside its content-rating system.

## 17. Current assessment

**🟢 Strongly favourable factors**

- No real money
- No cash prizes
- No cash-out
- No purchasable SC
- No exchangeable SC
- No sports betting
- No betting odds
- No casino mechanics
- No random gambling outcomes
- Football-data-driven valuations
- User trading volume cannot manipulate prices
- Gameplay is centred on scouting and portfolio strategy

**🟡 Area requiring clarification**

Whether Apple's or Google's definition of simulated gambling could
encompass the virtual portfolio mechanic despite the absence of
real-world value. This should be confirmed before launch rather than
assumed.

**🔴 No identified automatic deal-breaker at this stage**

Based on Scouta's currently defined mechanics, there is no identified
feature that obviously turns Scouta into a real-money gambling product.
This is not a guarantee of App Store approval — final classification and
approval remain with the relevant platform.

## 18. Design principles to preserve

Until the policy question has been resolved, Scouta should preserve
these principles:

1. SC must remain non-purchasable.
2. SC must have no real-world value.
3. SC must never be redeemable for cash.
4. No cash prizes should be introduced without a fresh policy/legal review.
5. Scouta should not introduce betting odds or match wagers.
6. Player prices should remain driven by football evidence rather than user betting volume.
7. Official product language should focus on scouting and football strategy.
8. Any future monetisation mechanic should be reviewed against App Store and Google Play policies before implementation.

## Bottom line

Scouta should be presented as a football scouting strategy game with a
virtual portfolio economy, not as a real-money investment or betting
product. The remaining uncertainty is not whether SC is real money (it
isn't) — it is whether platform policy treats a virtual portfolio
mechanic like this as "simulated gambling" regardless, which should be
confirmed with Apple/Google directly before launch.
